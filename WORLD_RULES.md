# Hontori: rules of the world

These rules come from the game's creator and are canon. Every feature, story beat and piece of content must follow them.
New rules are added here as they are given. When something in the code disagrees with this file, this file wins.

## Story and world
1. A kid lives with mom and dad. One night a spirit comes down the chimney: a fire hontori.
2. "Hontori" is the name of the kind (like a species), not a personal name. The player can give their hontori a name later.
3. The hontori speaks in short, gentle rhyme. Dialog is short, calm and nice. It does not try to be funny.
4. The world has magic. Ordinary people cannot see it; they are called the Veilbound.
5. The kid says their parents will freak. The hontori coughs up a magic wand and teaches the first spell, which lifts the veil (Velum Aperi, drawn as a V).
6. The human kid is the hero. Hontori are companions. The kid supports with spells; the hontori fight with their own attacks.
7. Before the adventure starts, the kid picks exactly 5 items from the house for the backpack.
8. The world has areas for each element. Each area has towns and a School of Magic.
9. There are three kinds of schools:
   - Small local schools: each teaches ONE spell. No ring. (Emberbrook Academy is the first: it teaches the Binding Spiral, the Element Wheel and practice duels.)
   - The 8 great schools of the mighty elements: their trial (a fight) awards a ring.
   - The archmage's school (the final boss): the last ring.
   Spells are never learned all at once; they are collected one school at a time.
10. Entering a School of Magic requires passing an entrance test.
11. Towns have a tavern to sleep and eat ramen (restores health and mana), a shop for magical and normal items (like backpacks), houses with people who have their own stories and small quests, and a school. Small quests help the bigger quest.
12. The big quest: defeat a revered, grandfatherly archmage-headmaster (original name, never an existing character), and make a new law so spirits are known by everyone and not seen as bad spirits.

## Elements
13. Eight elements: water, fire, wind, grass, electric, earth, dark, light.
14. Elements affect each other through a matrix (strong and weak). The matrix applies to every attack, both ways.
15. Weather and time of day boost or weaken hontori.
16. At the start of every battle, the game shows whether the matchup is an advantage or disadvantage.

## Hontori
17. Hontori look like fun animals. All designs and names are original: never an existing game creature or name.
18. Each hontori evolves 4 times (5 forms), from simple to epic, at levels 5, 10, 20 and 25.
19. Hontori have experience and levels. Each has its own attacks, learned as it levels up; the player picks which to bring, to avoid repetition.
20. Hontori appear where they make sense (water near water, dark at night, and so on), driven by habitat, time and weather.
21. Wild hontori can be jealous and attack the kid's companions.
22. Meeting hontori fills the wand's memory, which holds 100 different spirits and their evolutions.
23. The kid carries up to 5 hontori. Extra hontori are stored inside the wand (the Wand Sanctum).

## Magic and the wand
24. Spells are drawn with a gesture (mouse or finger) and must look epic and magical, not like children's shapes.
25. Magic is introduced bit by bit: healing, revival, stun, sleep, escape and more.
26. Revival needs materials, based on the spirit's element.
27. Spells cost mana. You cannot spam spells. Mana refills slowly, and from ramen and items.
28. How well a spell is drawn affects its strength.
29. The wand has experience and levels. As it levels up, spells get more efficient and do not need a perfect drawing.
30. The player chooses which spells to bring into battle (a loadout).
31. A hontori can only be captured when weakened, with a hard binding spell. The chance depends on its level and type. Winning a battle never makes one join by itself.
32. In battle, the kid can call another hontori to replace the active one.
33. Escaping a battle is a real choice with a chance, not just a button.

## Look and feel
34. Tile-based 2D in the spirit of classic handheld monster RPGs, but with better shading, lighting, wind, weather and time of day.
35. Clean and pleasant to the eye: big clear shapes, smooth shading, no noisy lines, no zig-zag layouts, symmetric buildings.
36. Rich, interactable world: everything can be interacted with. Building interiors are detailed and lived in.
37. NPCs have lively idle animations, and the same NPC look never appears twice close together.
38. Never use emojis anywhere.
39. Mobile first (portrait phone), also web. Joystick on the left, buttons on the right. No zoom on double tap. Works in Safari.
40. Fitting music.
41. Time flows on its own (one game hour every 2 real minutes, paused in dialogs, menus and battles), carries over when entering or leaving places, and sleeping skips 8 hours.
42. The kid has a map of the current area (map button next to the backpack).
43. You cannot capture another person's hontori: the owner pushes the binding away with their hand.
44. Teaching style: like classic handheld monster RPGs. Each mechanic is shown once, when it first matters, in one short line. Never over-explain, never leave anything unexplained.
45. How the game grows: always build toward the end of the story, step by step and world by world (finish and polish one area and its chapter before the next), staying consistent with everything already made.
46. Every hontori can be given its own name (the fox asks for one at the end of the intro); the name is used everywhere.
47. Home items: after the first morning the 12 home things lie around the house; the bag holds 5 (6 with the Backpack) and things can be put back while at home. Leaving without the Map, Mom hands it over (no slot). Each has one use: Map opens the map button; Lantern lights the night and draws dark hontori; Compass points to the goal; Bread calms a wild foe once per battle (+15% binding); Cookbook cooks at a stove (party heals by half); Teddy lets you rest 8 hours outdoors; Slingshot knocks one apple per fruit tree per day (+5 health); Scarf stops strong wind slowing you; Kite on a windy day in open grass reveals a large area of the map; Mirror shows wild hontori hidden as shimmer (they cannot be met without it); Candle gives +1 mana over max after sleeping; Backpack gives one more slot.
48. First touch helper: the first time any button, icon or system is used, a small neat helper card explains it in one or two short lines (shown once, can be skipped). If something is not available yet, it is shown but blocked, with one line saying where it is learned; the full explanation is given there.
49. The great schools do not all test the same way. Each ring trial is its own kind of challenge: a wand-magic trial, a boss fight, a fight with limits (no sleeping spell, no switching hontori), or a one-on-one duel between spell casters with no hontori. A fun mix, never eight copies of one fight.
50. Kids with open eyes and a wand are called Wonders (wand, wanderer, wonder). Grown-ups use the word kindly; guards use it for who may pass.
51. Hontori moves use stamina (ST). Only a real rest refills it: a bed, a night's sleep, ramen, or the teddy. The teddy works almost anywhere, never in schools, tournaments or dungeons.
52. Hontori evolve at levels 5, 10, 20 and 25 through an Element Bloom: the hontori curls into a shell of its element and the kid opens it with the V. Original look, nothing borrowed from other games.
53. Everything must be original and safe for app stores: no names, screens, sounds or phrases taken from other games, films or books (no Pokemon, Nintendo or Harry Potter echoes). The Academy rival is Cole, not Ash.
54. Store text, screenshots and ads never describe Hontori as "like Pokemon" or any other game. The genre (spirit companions, evolution, eight rings) is fine; borrowed names, looks and phrases are not. In-game words are our own: classmate, rival, Wonder, never trainer.
55. Every door that looks open can be entered, and every house has a real inside with its own people and things to find. A closed door always has a reason the player can learn (locked for the night, a family away, a story secret). This is the charm of the world.
56. The fox guide appears about half a second after the thing it explains, and it is placed where it covers nothing important (not the thing it points at, not buttons, cards or dialogs). It never blocks taps.
57. Fishing is magic too: a fisher (Old Oru in Emberbrook) teaches the Fishing Sigil once, a hook drawn in the air. With it the kid can fish in every water: rivers, ponds, streams, lakes, the sea. A cast costs 1 mana and brings a fish, a water hontori to calm, or only weeds. Each water has its own fish (some only at night) and its own hontori, at levels that fit the area. Fish can be sold or cooked. Fishers who already taught you offer a friendly contest instead, once a day.
58. Spells learned outside battle (the V, the Fishing Sigil) never take one of the four battle slots.
59. The player is never hidden: roads, entrances and arrival points never run behind a building or a tall object, and if the kid does step behind something tall, a soft see-through outline shows where they are.
60. Nothing exists only in words: anything the story says (a rockslide, a locked gate, a washed-out bridge, a closed shop) is also shown in the world, clearly, and tells the player what it is when they touch it.
61. Everything new respects the look of the world: same pixel style, palette, outline and scale as what is already there. Reuse the existing pieces (the wooden sign, the fences, the rocks) before drawing a new one, and a new piece must look like it always belonged.
