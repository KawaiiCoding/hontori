# Hontori: rules of the world

These rules come from the game's creator and are canon. Every feature, story beat and piece of content must follow them.
New rules are added here as they are given. When something in the code disagrees with this file, this file wins.

## Story and world
1. A kid lives with mom and dad. One night a spirit comes down the chimney: a fire hontori.
2. "Hontori" is the name of the kind (like a species), not a personal name. The player can give their hontori a name later.
3. The hontori speaks in short, gentle rhyme. Dialog is short, calm and nice. It does not try to be funny.
4. The world has magic. Ordinary people cannot see it; they are called the Veilbound.
5. The kid says their parents will freak. The hontori coughs up a magic wand and teaches the first spell, which lifts the veil (Velum Aperi, drawn as a V).
6. The human kid is the hero. Hontori are companions. The kid supports with spells; the hontori fight with their own attacks.
7. Before the adventure starts, the kid picks exactly 5 items from the house for the backpack.
8. The world has areas for each element. Each area has towns and a School of Magic.
9. There are three kinds of schools:
   - Small local schools: each teaches ONE spell. No ring. (Emberbrook Academy is the first: it teaches the Binding Spiral, the Element Wheel and practice duels.)
   - The 8 great schools of the mighty elements: their trial (a fight) awards a ring.
   - The archmage's school (the final boss): the last ring.
   Spells are never learned all at once; they are collected one school at a time.
10. Entering a School of Magic requires passing an entrance test.
11. Towns have a tavern to sleep and eat ramen (restores health and mana), a shop for magical and normal items (like backpacks), houses with people who have their own stories and small quests, and a school. Small quests help the bigger quest.
12. The big quest: defeat a revered, grandfatherly archmage-headmaster (original name, never an existing character), and make a new law so spirits are known by everyone and not seen as bad spirits.

## Elements
13. Eight elements: water, fire, wind, grass, electric, earth, dark, light.
14. Elements affect each other through a matrix (strong and weak). The matrix applies to every attack, both ways.
15. Weather and time of day boost or weaken hontori.
16. At the start of every battle, the game shows whether the matchup is an advantage or disadvantage.

## Hontori
17. Hontori look like fun animals. All designs and names are original: never an existing game creature or name.
18. Each hontori evolves 4 times (5 forms), from simple to epic.
19. Hontori have experience and levels. Each has its own attacks, learned as it levels up; the player picks which to bring, to avoid repetition.
20. Hontori appear where they make sense (water near water, dark at night, and so on), driven by habitat, time and weather.
21. Wild hontori can be jealous and attack the kid's companions.
22. Meeting hontori fills the wand's memory, which holds 100 different spirits and their evolutions.
23. The kid carries up to 5 hontori. Extra hontori are stored inside the wand (the Wand Sanctum).

## Magic and the wand
24. Spells are drawn with a gesture (mouse or finger) and must look epic and magical, not like children's shapes.
25. Magic is introduced bit by bit: healing, revival, stun, sleep, escape and more.
26. Revival needs materials, based on the spirit's element.
27. Spells cost mana. You cannot spam spells. Mana refills slowly, and from ramen and items.
28. How well a spell is drawn affects its strength.
29. The wand has experience and levels. As it levels up, spells get more efficient and do not need a perfect drawing.
30. The player chooses which spells to bring into battle (a loadout).
31. A hontori can only be captured when weakened, with a hard binding spell. The chance depends on its level and type. Winning a battle never makes one join by itself.
32. In battle, the kid can call another hontori to replace the active one.
33. Escaping a battle is a real choice with a chance, not just a button.

## Look and feel
34. Tile-based 2D in the spirit of classic handheld monster RPGs, but with better shading, lighting, wind, weather and time of day.
35. Clean and pleasant to the eye: big clear shapes, smooth shading, no noisy lines, no zig-zag layouts, symmetric buildings.
36. Rich, interactable world: everything can be interacted with. Building interiors are detailed and lived in.
37. NPCs have lively idle animations, and the same NPC look never appears twice close together.
38. Never use emojis anywhere.
39. Mobile first (portrait phone), also web. Joystick on the left, buttons on the right. No zoom on double tap. Works in Safari.
40. Fitting music.
41. Time of day carries over when entering or leaving places. Only sleeping moves time forward (8 hours).
42. The kid has a map of the current area (map button next to the backpack).
43. You cannot capture another person's hontori: the owner pushes the binding away with their hand.
